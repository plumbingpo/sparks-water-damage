Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Sparks Restoration know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

How We Handle Structural Drying

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
