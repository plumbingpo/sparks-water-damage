Bathroom Water Damage in Sparks

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Sparks, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
